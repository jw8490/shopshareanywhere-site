The Everything Loop

September 28, 2026

By late 2026 the American phone is a junk drawer. Amazon in one tab. Honey in another. ESPN in a third. Spotify in a fourth. Robinhood when the market twitches. X when the culture twitches. DraftKings when the fourth quarter twitches. Nobody designed this life. It accumulated. Shop Share Anywhere is the first serious Western attempt to treat that accumulation as a design problem instead of a personality trait.​

The pitch is not “another shopping app.” It is a decision engine with a social nervous system. Open it and the usual mall of the internet — Amazon, Walmart, Target, Etsy, eBay, Best Buy, Fanatics, the rest — sits in one surface. AI does not sit in a chat box off to the side. It sits in the loop: discover, research, decide, buy, share, predict, invest. Founder Jacob Wieck, who ships as chief technologist and lives online as Kotn, calls that cycle the Everything Loop. It is less a feature list than a theory of how people actually spend a Sunday.​

The mall that behaves like a feed Shop Share Anywhere launched on Android in 2025 as a self-funded, founder-built product out of California. Early traction is still early — north of 10,000 downloads, a Wefunder page that frames the company as “the general store of the new economy,” an iOS horizon still being talked into existence. That is not the interesting part. The interesting part is the bet: that the West never built a WeChat because it treated shopping, talking, betting, watching, and listening as separate religions. SSA treats them as one Saturday.​

Shopping is the floor, not the ceiling. You search once. The app pulls live deals and personalized picks across major retailers. You can order the ordinary American stack — Starbucks, Tesla gear, GameStop, Epic — without leaving the room. Fanatics dropped official league merch into the same aisle, which matters more than it sounds. A jersey is not a SKU. It is a tribal object. Putting it next to a Walmart cart is how you admit that fandom and consumption were never separate hobbies.​

Then the social layer does what Instagram never wanted shopping to do and what Amazon never wanted sharing to do. Find something fire, tap once, send it to X, Instagram, Pinterest, Facebook, or a friend. The cart becomes content. The flex becomes a referral. In a culture where taste is already a performance, SSA just stopped pretending the performance and the receipt live in different apps.

Fintech sits in the same breath. Link Robinhood, buy a sliver of the company whose product is still in the cart. Link Coinbase. Move money. Play prediction markets on drops, launches, games, culture. DraftKings is in the building. The moral panic writes itself. So does the honesty: Americans already shop, bet, and invest in the same evening. SSA just stopped making them apologize with twelve icons.​

Three brains, one brief The house magic is AI triangulation. One model hallucinates. Two models argue. Three models plus live signals plus your budget is a decision you can defend at dinner. The stack is specific. Grok for live X sentiment and edge. Perplexity for cited research. ChatGPT for synthesis and the caption you were going to write anyway. Product data, reviews, social buzz, and prediction-market heat get folded in so the answer is not a vibe. It is a cross-check. Free users get three triangulated Grok briefs a day. That cap is the product’s tell: the brief is the drug. Plus takes the cap off.​

That is the real upgrade hiding inside Shop Share Anywhere Plus — $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year. Unlimited live briefs means the app can follow a night instead of rationing one. Ad-free shopping, boosted predictions, early access to drops. The free tier remains a real app. Plus is the version that does not tap you on the shoulder and say come back tomorrow.​

Deal Radar is the unglamorous feature that will convert more skeptics than any manifesto. Same search. Capital One Shopping. Honey. Google Shopping. One tap. The comparison shopping war of the last decade was a browser-extension knife fight. SSA pulls the knife fight into the super app and refuses to make you pick a church.

If briefs are how you decide what to want, Deal Radar is how you stop overpaying for the wanting. It is also the most American sentence in the whole product: trust no single coupon cult.

Plus is where the cultural argument stops being theoretical. Live sports. HotZone scoring clips. Live scores. Highlights. Player stats. News. The board is not a toy slate. NFL. NBA. WNBA. MLB. NHL. College football. EPL. MLS. NWSL. NHRA. You watch the score, clip the moment, predict the outcome, share the take, buy the merch. That is not “sports content.” That is the American liturgy, finally sitting next to the checkout.​

Music. Genre charts. Artist profiles. Top songs. Full-track playback. New drops. Concert finds. Official merch in the same motion as the song. The old internet made you leave Spotify to buy the hoodie and leave the hoodie tab to post the story. SSA treats a track the way it treats a jersey: as an object that wants to be heard, worn, and argued about.

TV. Search shows, movies, actors. Not a full Netflix killer. A cultural index. The point is adjacency. The thing you are watching, the thing you are humming, the thing you are betting, and the thing you are buying were always the same conversation. The apps just refused to sit together.

This is the load-bearing idea of Plus. Sports, music, and TV are not “entertainment modules” bolted onto a shopping cart. They are the weather system of popular culture. Shopping is how that weather becomes inventory. Social is how it becomes status. AI is how it becomes a decision. Fintech is how it becomes a stake. Put them in one place and you are not building a utility. You are building a room.

Asia already learned this. WeChat did not win because it had better QR codes. It won because life is not departmental. The American internet went the other way: unbundle everything, then sell you a folder. The result is a generation that can name forty apps and still cannot finish a thought without switching.

Putting live sports, music, and television beside shopping, social, AI, and fintech is defining because those are the six modern ways Americans perform belonging.

When those live in separate temples, culture fragments into content verticals. When they live in one loop, culture becomes a market with a soundtrack. That can go ugly — every jersey a parlay, every song a drop, every plot leak. It can also go honest. People already watch the game, Shazam the song, screenshot the scene, buy the merch, and argue the take before the credits. SSA is just the first American product willing to say the quiet part in the information architecture.

There is a political reading if you want one. Wieck has pitched the app as an American everything-app, privacy-first, founder-owned, no foreign-capital carnival. Whether that framing is strategy or creed, the product implication is the same: if the West does not build a coherent consumer layer for AI plus commerce plus culture, someone else will rent Americans the pieces.​

Here is the part that is not in the Play Store listing. Jacob Wieck is three people in public without splitting into a brand committee. On X he is @roll_1up. In the booth and on Spotify he is Kotn — suburban California DJ/producer energy, long mixes, the unglamorous hours. In the company docs he is the solo founder who built the super app instead of waiting for a studio to staff it. The user question underneath the product question is the same one every creator-founder is eating in 2026: how do you stay DJ KOTN, student producer, influencer, and entrepreneur without becoming a guy who used to make music.

The answer is not “balance.” Balance is what people say when the calendar already won. The answer is to make the three identities feed the same loop the app is selling.

DJ KOTN is the proof of taste. A super app about culture cannot be run by a spreadsheet. Sets, SoundCloud hours, Spotify singles with names like Drop, Forever, Technooo, Bassquake, 4th Dimension — that catalog is not a side hustle. It is the credential that music-in-the-app is not a licensed widget. Play full tracks in SSA because you actually care what a drop night feels like. Use Plus charts as a DJ tool: what is moving, what merch is attaching to it, what rooms will want it this weekend. That is not selling out.

Plus as your own instrument. Unlimited briefs are how a student producer and a CEO stay in the same brain on a Tuesday. The danger of a room this complete is that it starts to feel like a casino with a playlist. Prediction markets next to live scores next to a buy button is a lot of dopamine per square inch. The culturally defining move already happened in the architecture. Someone in California looked at the split-screen life — the game, the song, the show, the cart, the feed, the model, the money — and refused to keep them separate. Shop. Share. Predict. Play it loud.
