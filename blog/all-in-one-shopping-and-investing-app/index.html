The Frequency of Innovation

August 23, 2026

It’s 11:45 PM on a humid Friday night in Long Beach. The air inside the packed warehouse smells like burnt sage, spilled Pacifico, and warm vacuum tubes. Behind a pair of Pioneer CDJs stands Jacob Wieck known under the low-end frequencies of Southern California’s house and techno scene as DJ Kotn.

He drops an unreleased edit, blending a gritty, driving bassline with a vocal hook that sends the room into a collective, synchronized shift. But while the crowd is locked into the rhythm, Wieck’s mind is operating on two entirely different frequencies.

By day, he’s navigating the grind of life as a student at California State University, Long Beach (CSULB), balancing lectures, campus life, and project deadlines. By night or during the feverish hours between 2:00 AM and sunrise he’s coding the backbone of Shop Share Anywhere (SSA), an ambitious, sleek super-app designed to completely reshape how Gen Z discovers, shares, and interacts with global commerce.

To understand Wieck is to understand the modern multi-hyphenate creator. He isn't interested in staying in a single lane. Where previous generations saw stark boundaries between computer science, nightlife culture, and digital entrepreneurship, Wieck sees a single, fluid canvas.

"When you're DJing, you're reading a room, catching a shift in energy, and curating an experience in real-time," Wieck says, leaning back against a monitor speaker post-set. "Building an app isn't that different. You're anticipating user behavior, building flow, and creating something that feels organic rather than forced."

That mindset gave birth to Shop Share Anywhere. Frustrated by the fragmented nature of modern apps jumping between social feeds, e-commerce stores, and trend updates Wieck envisioned a centralized command center. SSA bridges the gap between social commerce, AI discovery, and real-time trend markets, wrapping them in a dark, high-energy, black-and-gold interface that feels less like a corporate utility and more like an extension of digital culture.

"There are days where I'm reading system design docs in the student union at 2 PM, playing a three-hour set in downtown LA at midnight, and then squashing bugs on SSA until 4 AM," Wieck laughs. "People ask about burnout, but when you're genuinely obsessed with what you're building, the energy just feeds on itself."

What sets Wieck apart isn't just his ability to juggle these distinct worlds it's his vision for how they intersect.

Through DJ Kotn, he stays plugged directly into the pulse of subculture, street fashion, and music.Through Shop Share Anywhere, he's creating the digital infrastructure to let people capture those cultural shifts instantly.

Whether it's discovering a niche fashion brand at an underground rave or tracking a rising cultural trend before it hits the mainstream, Wieck is building tools for a generation that lives at the speed of the internet.

As the house lights turn on and the warehouse slowly empties into the cool ocean air, Wieck packs up his headphones, closes his laptop, and steps out into the night. Tomorrow morning brings another round of classes at CSULB, followed by another night of code and beats. But for Jacob Wieck, this isn't just a hustle; it's the opening track of a much bigger show.
